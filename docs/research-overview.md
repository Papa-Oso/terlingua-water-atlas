# Research overview

**Evidence reviewed through October 23, 2026.** This is a snapshot of public records, not a live report of water availability or construction progress.

The central question is how existing sources, treatment and distribution could support more reliable water access around Terlingua and Study Butte. Public records already describe meaningful work on that problem. The next useful contribution is to connect those records, identify what they establish, and narrow the measurements still needed.

## Groundwater research already exists

A February 2026 presentation by hydrogeologist Steve Finch describes a conceptual model for Santa Elena Limestone and southern Brewster County. It separates the region into Solitario, Terlingua Ranch and Glen Rose zones, with different geology, inferred recharge pathways and water quality. It provides a framework for comparing wells by geological setting as well as distance. [Brewster County GCD presentation](https://www.brewstercountygcd.com/s/Hydrogeologic-Conceptual-Model-of-Santa-Elena-Limestone-and-South-Brewster-County_18Feb2026.pdf), especially slides 13–16, 18, 23 and 27.

The presentation leaves significant characterization gaps. It does not quantify additional drought-reliable pumping or demonstrate a particular new well's expected yield. Its conceptual boundaries are regional interpretations. Locating supporting datasets, any final report and later follow-up work is more useful than starting another introductory study without checking what already exists.

## Existing infrastructure projects provide a starting point

TWDB project 62844 has a documented combined budget of **$1.48 million**: an earlier $300,000 commitment and $1.18 million approved in December 2025. The later approval describes replacement of old mains, improved controls and rehabilitation of storage. These are components of the same project budget, not amounts to add again. [December 2025 board memo](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/board/2025/12/Board/Brd36.pdf).

The utility's published environmental document describes 7,550 feet of main replacement along highways 118 and 170, control-system work and two 35,000-gallon tanks. It excludes the Ron Read Road alternate from that environmental clearance. This is evidence of planned rehabilitation, not proof of new regional service capacity or completed construction. [Utility project page](https://www.studybuttewater.com/copy-of-about), [environmental document](https://www.studybuttewater.com/_files/ugd/313504_c08d56c07c694d8eb60b3e4e646bb474.pdf).

A separate April 2026 county notice describes replacement of one reverse-osmosis treatment system and associated equipment, with an estimated **$750,000 in HUD funds and $15,000 in other funds**. The notice concerns an intended request for release of funds; it does not establish completion or treatment headroom. [Brewster County notice](https://www.brewstercounty.gov/upload/page/0075/Brewster%20County%20CDV25-0401%20FONSI.pdf).

Together, these records support investigating improvements that complement existing work. They do not yet establish a price or supply volume for serving the whole region.

## Actual water-use reports sharpen the question

TWDB's historical reports for the Study Butte/Terlingua Water System, survey 0827151, provide the following annual figures:

| Reporting year | Raw groundwater intake | Metered retail water | Reported connections |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024 | 67,858,131 gallons | 19,876,700 gallons | 298 |
| 2025 | 69,861,006 gallons | 20,163,600 gallons | 300 |

Sources: [2024 water-use report](https://www3.twdb.texas.gov/apps/reports/WU/Historical_FinalWaterUseSurvey?year=2024&surveyno=0827151&rs:Command=Render), [2025 water-use report](https://www3.twdb.texas.gov/apps/reports/WU/Historical_FinalWaterUseSurvey?year=2025&surveyno=0827151&rs:Command=Render). These links open the public report viewer; its PDF export contains the relevant table. The figures were checked against the exported reports.

The gap between raw intake and retail deliveries is a concrete accounting question. It is **not a leakage estimate**: treatment reject water, process use, other uses and accounting boundaries must be reconciled with a water balance or water-loss audit. Neither annual figure establishes rated capacity, spare water or sustainable aquifer yield.

## Research priorities

1. **Reconcile the water balance.** Connect raw pumping, treated production, treatment reject, authorized uses and distribution losses using compatible dates and units.
2. **Track the existing projects.** Match funding, scope, design, contracts and completion records before proposing overlapping work.
3. **Test the source constraint.** Connect the conceptual groundwater model to sustained pumping tests, water levels, chemistry and interactions between wells.
4. **Define the service gap.** Establish which residents and businesses are underserved, their demand, and which combinations of pipes, storage and lawful fill or delivery services could meet it.
5. **Improve the evidence map.** Reconcile duplicate wells and geological units before attempting predictions. Preserve unknown outcomes and location uncertainty.

## Collection limits

The catalog is an inventory of sources located during a targeted public search. It is not an exhaustive archive of everything ever released. Some meeting attachments were inaccessible, some records were available only offline, and some scanned documents have not been fully searched or reviewed. An agenda establishes scheduled consideration; approved minutes, contracts and completion records answer different questions.

Review status and source limitations accompany the catalog and findings. A record missing from this collection may still exist elsewhere. No conclusion about current source availability, spare utility capacity or project completion should be inferred from that absence.
